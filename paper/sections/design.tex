\section{Experimental design}\label{sec:design}

\subsection{A semi-synthetic twin}\label{sec:twin}
% Source: docs/analysis_plan.md §14 item 19 and its amendments of 2026-11-16 and 2026-11-20
% (src/models/twin.py). Written before any real-data arm was scored.

On real data the true drivers of a head are unknown, so neither the value of the graph's
knowledge nor the reasons behind a model's accuracy can be checked. A semi-synthetic twin supplies
both. A \emph{world} keeps everything real (the wells, the weeks, the weather, the facility
volumes, the river and canal series, the pattern of missing heads and the splits) except the heads,
which are synthetic: each head is a level plus the sum, over recharge, pumping, river and canal, of
a gain times a response kernel convolved with the input that a \emph{truth graph} routes to the
well, plus autocorrelated noise. Recharge, river and canal edges of the truth are arm~D's. For
pumping, two dials set how far the graph is from the truth: a share $\varepsilon$ of the graph's
attributed facility--well pairs are false, and a share $u$ of each well's true pumping volume
comes from pairs that the graph does not attribute. The learner never sees the truth graph; its
arms C and D are exactly the tables the real-data runs use, and a third arm, T, fits the learner on
the truth's own routing: the oracle that bounds what routing could gain.

The responses of the truth are gamma kernels whose mean lags are drawn around the published
responses of this aquifer (9 to 31 weeks for recharge and about 11 weeks for seasonal pumping;
\citealp{Caballero2015Impact}). The gains give each contribution a share of the real well's
training variance: $\pi$ for pumping, 0.1 for a river or a canal where the truth has the edge, and
the rest for recharge. The noise is AR(1), with a variance $\nu$ times that of the noise-free head.
The base world has $\pi = 0.3$ and $\nu = 0.25$. The grid crosses $\varepsilon \in \{0, 0.1, 0.25,
0.5\}$ with $u \in \{0, 0.3, 0.6\}$, 20 worlds per cell. Measurements made before any arm was
scored place the real graph between $\varepsilon = 0.08$ (one minus the measured precision of
the attribution rule) and 0.37 (one minus its precision at exact-unit resolution) and near $u = 0.66$ (the graph
routes about a third of the groundwater volume within \SI{5}{km} of the core wells), so the four
cells with $\varepsilon \in \{0.1, 0.25\}$ and $u \in \{0.3, 0.6\}$ bracket the real data. Slices
from the cell $(\varepsilon, u) = (0.1, 0.3)$ change one thing at a time: the pumping signal
$\pi$, the noise $\nu$, the timing of pumping (independent of the year's weather), hidden variation
in the years whose volumes are imputed, an omitted driver (a slow random walk shared by one aquifer
group), responses the model does not have (recharge above a threshold, pumping with a dead time),
and a graph that is wrong about recharge, rivers and canals, not only pumping.

Each world is fitted by the response model of \cref{sec:models} for arms C, D and T, from the
same two starts, under two fitting protocols: early stopping on the validation period, and
convergence with weak pooling of the parameters across wells ($\lambda = 0.1$, 3,000 steps).
Six scores are computed per world and arm, on the core wells in the drought phase: (1) the median
over wells of $\log(\mathrm{RMSE}_D / \mathrm{RMSE}_C)$, the primary measure of the real-data
contrasts; (2) the recovery error of each driver's contribution, the RMSE between the fitted and
the true contribution in units of the well's noise-free standard deviation; (3) the share of wells
whose fitted pumping contribution follows the true one; (4) the error of the mean lag; (5) the
response to an intervention, a \SI{20}{\percent} cut of every true facility of one well in the
drought years; and (6) whether the world has \emph{wrong reasons}: an arm whose pumping recovery
error exceeds 0.5 while its drought efficiency is within 0.1 of the oracle's. The rules that read
the map were fixed before any world was read. A cell \emph{pays} when the median of score~1 over
its worlds is at most $-0.05$, the smallest effect of interest proposed for the real-data contrasts,
and its 97.5\,\% quantile is below zero. % SESOI: Gate 3 point 3

\subsection{Real-data contrasts}\label{sec:contrasts}
% Sources: docs/phase4_runs.md; docs/gate3_decisions.md (points 1–4, 6–14, 18–21);
% docs/analysis_plan.md §4, §8, §10, §14 items 18, 20, 23. Written before any Phase 4 run.

Eight pre-registered blocks (B1--B8) plus a converged explanation block (B2conv) share one pooled
response-model backbone (\cref{sec:models}), so each primary contrast varies one factor. RQ1 tests
knowledge in two forms: as \emph{information} in B1, comparing knowledge-graph facts plus dataset
attributes (arm~B) against dataset attributes alone (arm~A) as static inputs at rung~3
(\cref{sec:ladder}), read mainly on left-out wells; and as \emph{structure}, primarily by routing
in B2 (arm~D's knowledge-graph links against arm~C's distance rules at rung~4) and secondarily by
message passing at rung~5 in B5. Two controls guard RQ1: B1c replaces arm~B's facts with random
name tags or shuffled facts drawn per start \citep{Heudorfer2024On}; B3 replaces arm~D's edges with
degree-preserving rewiring ($E_{\mathrm{degree}}$), distance-matched rewiring ($E_{\mathrm{distance}}$,
a locality control for pumping only), random graphs ($F_{\mathrm{random}}$, five draws each) and
the complete graph ($F_{\mathrm{complete}}$).

RQ2 is tested on arm~D in B4: hard signs by construction (recharge $\ge 0$, attributed and
unattributed pumping $\le 0$, river $\ge 0$ at the three eligible wells, canal free;
\cref{sec:signs}), no signs (none), and soft penalties on the declared grid
$\lambda_s \in \{1, 10, 100\}$. Per reconstruction, validation selects the strongest $\lambda_s$
whose R1 median core NSE lies within one same-arm band half-width of the best grid score (reused in
R2), reporting all three as a dose--response. Three sensitivity blocks follow: B6 reruns D versus C
with confident pump links only and with all links alongside the main policy (\cref{sec:kg-export});
B7 replaces arm~D's main recharge input (rain minus a fitted share of evapotranspiration over local
outcrops) with unit-wide outcrops, soil drainage, or effective rain as is, and adds a soft canal
prior ($\lambda_s = 10$); B8 adds a bounded fixed seasonal residual (day-of-year harmonics, seeing no
stress so hard signs hold) with and without on rungs~3--5.

Contrasts span R1, R2 (\nDataFolds{} grouped leave-wells-out folds), R3 (\SI{25}{\percent} and
\SI{50}{\percent} windows) and a train-plus-validation refit in B1, B1c and B2 (R1--R2 in B3--B6;
R1 in B2conv, B7, B8; \cref{sec:periods}). Primary blocks (B1, B2, B2conv, B4) run under all three
pumping reconstructions of \cref{sec:inputs}; a conclusion must hold under all three. Two fitting
protocols carry fixed roles: early stopping on validation ($\lambda = 0.01$) for accuracy contrasts
(B1--B8), and converged pooling ($\lambda = 0.1$, 3,000 fixed steps; B2conv) for explanation claims
(RQ3--RQ4; \cref{sec:twin}). Every run uses 10 starts on one machine type for every arm of a
contrast. Confirmatory families, pre-specified on the drought benchmark, are RQ1 (B versus A, D
versus C) and RQ2 (soft versus none, hard versus none); all other contrasts (B1c, B3, B5--B8,
secondary regimes, subgroups) are exploratory.

\subsection{Metrics and statistics}\label{sec:metrics}
% Sources: docs/analysis_plan.md §1, §2, §6, §14 items 18, 23; docs/gate3_decisions.md (points 3, 14);
% src/analysis/stats.py; src/dataset/placeholders.py. Written before any Phase 4 run.

The primary contrast measure is, per core well (\nDataCoreWells{} wells), the log ratio of
drought-phase RMSEs, $\log(\mathrm{RMSE}_a / \mathrm{RMSE}_b)$ (negative favours arm~$a$),
summarised by its median over core wells. Because NSE and skill differences always share sign,
testing one ratio avoids splitting multiplicity; median NSE \citep{Nash1970River}, skill against
training climatology \citep{Schaefli2007Do}, datum-free KGE$''$ \citep{Tang2021Sc} and RMSE remain
in every table as descriptions. Drought and recovery phases follow \cref{sec:periods}
(\cref{tab:periods}); recovery scores remain secondary and provisional.

Spatial dependence is handled by grouping core wells into \baselineClusterCount{} Lambert-93 cells
of \SI{10}{km}. Effect sizes come first: the median paired log RMSE ratio and its \SI{95}{\percent}
cluster-bootstrap interval (4,000 resamples). Hypotheses use an exact two-sided cluster sign-flip
test over all sign patterns of the \baselineClusterCount{} clusters (smallest attainable $p = 2/2^{14} \approx 1.2\times10^{-4}$),
Holm-adjusted within each confirmatory family (two tests per family). A claim requires
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.05$ and a bootstrap interval excluding zero; grid-size and shift
sensitivities accompany the \SI{10}{km} test.

The smallest effect of interest is \SI{5}{\percent} less error (log ratio $-0.05$), classifying
each primary contrast as detected, compatible with no effect larger than the interval's end, or
undecided. Beside each primary contrast sits its same-arm band from random disjoint five-start
halves of the 10 starts (both arms pooled); differences inside it are indistinguishable from
training noise. Every contrast requires paired support (the same wells and weeks for both arms,
strictly enforced for primary contrasts), and runs of at least 28 days at depth \SI{0.00}{m} are
masked as placeholder weeks from every scored variant (one core well, 60 drought weeks), with the
unmasked primary table also reported.
