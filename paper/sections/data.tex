\section{Study area and data}\label{sec:data}

\subsection{The Roussillon multilayer aquifer}\label{sec:site}

The Roussillon plain lies on the Mediterranean coast of southern France, in the Pyr\'en\'ees-Orientales,
between the Corbi\`eres to the north and the Albera range on the Spanish border (\cref{fig:site}). Three
rivers cross it to the sea, the Agly in the north, the T\^et in the middle and the Tech in the south,
with the smaller R\'eart between the last two. Under the plain lies a multilayer aquifer
\citep{Caballero2015Impact}. At the top, the recent alluvium of the four rivers and older alluvial
terraces form shallow porous aquifers. Below them, the Pliocene sands and clays form a multilayer
aquifer system, about \SI{902}{km^2} under the plain of which
\SI{289}{km^2} crop out. % step06b_bdlisa.md, "The plain's vertical structure"
To the north, the Pliocene rests on the karst of the Corbi\`eres.

Water does not flow one way between these layers. In 17 pairs of wells less than \SI{5}{km} apart,
one in a shallow and one in a deeper unit, the shallow head was above the deep one in only
\SI{50.5}{\percent} of the weeks they share, and ten pairs keep one ordering, in both directions: at a
pair \SI{20}{m} apart in the T\^et valley the water goes down in every week, while at a pair
\SI{50}{m} apart near the Agly it goes up from the Pliocene in \SI{94.5}{\percent} of weeks.
% step06b_bdlisa.md, "The key number" (17 pairs, 17,124 pair-weeks)
Four Pliocene wells are at times artesian. A constraint such as ``water infiltrates downward'' would
therefore be wrong about half of the time here; the knowledge used in this study constrains the
direction of a head's response to its drivers instead (\cref{sec:kg-module}), which holds everywhere.

The two water systems of the plain are almost separate. Over 2012--2023, \SI{99}{\percent} of the
declared drinking water came from groundwater, while \SI{84}{\percent} of the declared irrigation
came from surface water (\SI{93}{\percent} when the canal intakes are counted as irrigation). % step06a_bnpe.md, "Results: plain, cumulative 2012–2023"
Declarations cover only abstractions above a threshold, so groundwater irrigation is a lower bound.
The plain reached historic low groundwater levels in 2022--2024 and recovered in 2025--2026; that
drought is the test period of this study (\cref{sec:periods}).

\subsection{Heads, forcings, abstraction, rivers and canals}\label{sec:inputs}

\Cref{tab:data-sources} lists the data. The target is the weekly head of \nDataWells{} monitoring wells
of the d\'epartement \citep{Hubeau2026Piezometry}, the weekly mean of the daily values, after removing the
values whose quality their producer qualifies as incorrect or undeterminable. \nDataCoreWells{} of them form the core set on which every
result is scored; the others may be used for training. The weather comes from the SAFRAN reanalysis
\citep{Vidal2009A} and the SIM surface model \citep{Habets2008The} on an \SI{8}{km} grid
\citep{MeteoFrance2026Sim}: rain, potential and actual evaporation, effective rain, drainage, runoff,
temperature and a soil wetness index, summed or averaged by week, from 2000.

Abstraction comes from the annual declarations of the national water-abstraction database (BNPE)
\citep{Hubeau2026Abstraction}: \nDataGroundwaterPoints{} groundwater and \nDataSurfacePoints{} surface
abstraction points, with their use (irrigation, drinking water, canals, industry, energy). A weekly
series is needed, so each annual volume is spread over its year. In the main reconstruction,
irrigation and canal intakes follow the year's April--October climatic deficit and drinking water
is half flat and half seasonal; two other reconstructions (flat drinking water, and irrigation
following a fixed crop calendar) serve as sensitivities, and a conclusion that depends on the
reconstruction says so. Inside a year, the spread volumes are almost collinear with the climatic
deficit ($r = 0.92$--$0.97$), so the effects of pumping can only be learned from differences
between years and between wells. Years after the last published declaration are imputed and
marked.

River discharge comes from the gauges of the national hydrometric network
\citep{Hubeau2026Hydrometry}: the models use \nDataGaugesUsed{} gauges on the main rivers, as
$\log(1 + Q)$. Gaps of up to eight weeks are interpolated; longer gaps take the week-of-year median
of the training years, so that no input borrows from a later period. The canals enter as one series,
the weekly volume taken by the canal intakes of the plain. Land use comes from the annual
declarations of the graphical parcel register (RPG), \nDataRpgEditions{} editions, mapped to
crop classes with FAO-56 crop coefficients; it is used by the knowledge graph and by one
pre-registered test of a crop-demand input, which found no detectable information. The aquifer
units, their stacks and their outcrops come from the BD~LISA database, and the rivers and canals as
lines from the BD~TOPO.

\subsection{Periods and evaluation}\label{sec:periods}

The task is simulation: a model reads weather, abstraction, river and static inputs and never an
observed head. \Cref{tab:periods} gives the periods and \cref{fig:periods} the observed levels. Training ends in 2019; the validation period
is the only one used for any choice; the test period covers the drought and the recovery, and the
boundary between them follows a rule on observed levels only, never on a model's output: the recovery
starts in the first month after the trough from which the median standardised groundwater index
(SGI) of the core wells stays at or above $-0.5$ for three months. The primary endpoint is the
median over the core wells of the Nash--Sutcliffe efficiency \citep{Nash1970River} in the drought,
with the skill against the training climatology as a co-primary endpoint, because the efficiency of
a sub-period uses that sub-period's mean as its benchmark \citep{Schaefli2007Do}. A well is scored
in a period only with at least 26 observed weeks there.

Three regimes test different kinds of generalisation. In R1 every training well trains on the
training period. In R2 the core wells of one fold are left out of training; the five folds group
wells less than \SI{2}{km} apart \citep{Roberts2017Crossvalidation} and balance the aquifer groups,
and a left-out well's head is converted to metres with its own training mean and standard deviation,
so R2 measures the dynamics of a monitored well that the model did not see, not prediction at an
unmonitored place. In R3 the models train on the most recent \SI{25}{\percent} or \SI{50}{\percent} of
the training period.
