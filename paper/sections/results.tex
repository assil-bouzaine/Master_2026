\subsection{Where knowledge pays: the twin's map}\label{sec:res-twin}
% Numbers: \twin... macros of paper/tables/twin_numbers.tex (paper/tools/fig_twin_map.py, from
% results/v4/twin_map, 240 worlds per protocol); the reading is docs/results_twin_v4.md.
% The slices and RQ2 (kernel version 13) are added when pulled.

No cell of the grid pays, under either protocol (\cref{fig:twin-map}; \twinPaysFree{} and
\twinPaysPooled{} of \twinCells{} cells). The graph's accuracy gain
over distance routing stays within a few thousandths of a log unit: the cell medians of
$\log(\mathrm{RMSE}_D / \mathrm{RMSE}_C)$ range from $\twinDCMinFree$ to $\twinDCMaxFree$ under early
stopping and from $\twinDCMinPooled$ to $\twinDCMaxPooled$ under converged pooling, and every cell's
97.5\,\% quantile is above zero (the smallest is $\twinDCQHighMinPooled$).
This is not because the graph is too wrong. Even the oracle, which routes the true pumping, reduces
the RMSE of distance routing by only \twinTCGainMinFree{} to \twinTCGainMaxPooled\,\% (cell medians of
$\log(\mathrm{RMSE}_T / \mathrm{RMSE}_C)$ between $\twinTCMaxFree$ and $\twinTCMinPooled$). Where pumping carries \SI{30}{\percent} of the head's training variance and its weekly
timing follows the weather, knowing which facilities reach a well can improve the simulated head only a
little. A real-data D versus C contrast without a detected gain is therefore the expected outcome
in the four cells that bracket the real graph, not evidence that the graph is wrong.

The graph matters more for the reasons than for the accuracy, but only under one protocol. Under
converged pooling, arm~D recovers the pumping contribution better than arm~C in \twinRightMinPooled{} to
\twinRightMaxPooled\,\% of the worlds of a cell (\twinRightMeanPooled\,\% on average over cells); under
early stopping, in only \twinRightMinFree{} to \twinRightMaxFree\,\% (\twinRightMeanFree\,\%).
One reading is that early stopping halts the fit before the model separates pumping from the
seasonal weather that moves with it, so the routing hardly matters, while converged pooling lets
the routing decide how the head's summer decline is shared between the drivers. In absolute terms, early stopping recovers arm~D's
pumping better in \twinFreeBetterCells{} of the \twinCellsCompared{} cells and simulates the drought
slightly better (median drought efficiency $\twinNSEDiff$). This motivates pairing the protocols rather than choosing one: early
stopping for the accuracy contrasts and converged pooling for the explanation claims, declared
before the real-data runs. % pending Gate 3 point 1 In the base grid no world has wrong reasons for either arm (score~6: \twinWrongDFree{} of \twinWorldsFree{}
per protocol and arm), as expected where the generator lies inside the model's class; the slices with an
omitted driver and responses the model does not have are where wrong reasons can appear.

\subsection{Baselines and the forecasting reference}\label{sec:res-baselines}
% Numbers: \baseline... macros of paper/tables/baselines_numbers.tex (paper/tools/baseline_tables.py,
% from src/analysis/baseline_report.py on v4); full tables are docs/results_baselines_v4.md.

All baseline results are exploratory, because the test period was examined during model development
and error diagnosis. \Cref{tab:baselines-simulation} reports the simulation baselines over the
\nDataCoreWells{} core wells. In the drought phase, the climatology floor has a median NSE of
\baselineClimatologyDroughtNSE{} (zero skill by definition). The hydrology reference of record,
Pastas \citep{Collenteur2019Pastas} built from training heads only with each well's start chosen by
the frozen training-RMSE rule, reaches drought NSE \baselinePastasDroughtNSE{} and skill
\baselinePastasDroughtSkill{}. Every tuned learned baseline improves on both: the five single
architectures span drought NSE \baselineEALSTMDroughtNSE{} (EA-LSTM) to \baselineLSTMDroughtNSE{}
(LSTM; distance-graph ST-GNN \baselineDistanceSTGNNDroughtNSE{}) and skill
\baselineEALSTMDroughtSkill{} to \baselineLSTMDroughtSkill{} (distance-graph ST-GNN
\baselineDistanceSTGNNDroughtSkill{}), while the validation-selected ensemble reaches drought NSE
\baselineEnsembleDroughtNSE{} (skill \baselineEnsembleDroughtSkill{}).

The Pastas reference is sensitive to its optimisation starting values. Refitted per well from
\baselinePastasStarts{} starting values (training heads only), its core-well median drought NSE
spans \baselinePastasStartMinDroughtNSE{} to \baselinePastasStartMaxDroughtNSE{}, with a median
per-well range of \baselinePastasWellRangeMedian{}; the frozen rule yields
\baselinePastasDroughtNSE{}. In the spatially clustered sign-flip test over
\baselineClusterCount{} grid cells of \SI{10}{km} (Holm-adjusted across models), only the ensemble
outperforms the reference of record on drought NSE (median paired difference
\baselineEnsembleDiffDroughtNSE{}, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = \baselineEnsemblePHolmDroughtNSE$; on
drought skill both the ensemble, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = \baselineEnsemblePHolmDroughtSkill$, and the
distance-graph ST-GNN, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = \baselineDistanceSTGNNPHolmDroughtSkill$, pass).
Repeating the drought-NSE cluster test against each of the \baselinePastasStarts{} individual
starts confirms this: the ensemble passes ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.05$) against
\baselineEnsembleStartsPass{} of \baselinePastasStarts{} starts
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \le \baselineEnsembleStartsMaxPHolm$), whereas the five tuned single models
pass against only \baselineSingleStartsPassMin{} to \baselineSingleStartsPassMax{} of the
\baselinePastasStarts{} starts.

Across the generalisation regimes, leaving out monitored wells in the five spatial folds (R2)
hurts every tuned model sharply: median drought NSE drops to between
\baselineRegimeTwoMinDroughtNSE{} and \baselineRegimeTwoMaxDroughtNSE{}, and drought skill to
between \baselineRegimeTwoMinDroughtSkill{} and \baselineRegimeTwoMaxDroughtSkill{}. By contrast,
training on \SI{50}{\percent} or \SI{25}{\percent} of the training period (R3) costs little,
leaving drought skill between \baselineRegimeThreeMinDroughtSkill{} and
\baselineRegimeThreeMaxDroughtSkill{} and drought NSE between \baselineRegimeThreeMinDroughtNSE{}
and \baselineRegimeThreeMaxDroughtNSE{}.

\Cref{tab:baselines-forecast} turns to the forecasting hindcast, where past observed heads up to
the issue week are available alongside reconstructed forcings and predictions are scored at
\num{4} and \num{12} weeks against anomaly persistence. Chronos-2 \citep{Ansari2025Chronos} leads at
both horizons during the drought: at \num{4} weeks, \baselineChronosFourVariant{} reaches drought
NSE \baselineChronosFourDroughtNSE{} (skill \baselineChronosFourDroughtSkill{}), ahead of anomaly
persistence (\baselineAnomalyPersistenceFourDroughtNSE{}) and LightGBM
(\baselineLightGBMFourDroughtNSE{}); at \num{12} weeks, \baselineChronosTwelveVariant{} reaches
\baselineChronosTwelveDroughtNSE{} (skill \baselineChronosTwelveDroughtSkill{}), against
\baselineAnomalyPersistenceTwelveDroughtNSE{} for anomaly persistence and
\baselineLightGBMTwelveDroughtNSE{} for LightGBM. These scores set the forecasting reference when
recent heads are known; they do not address the head-free simulation task for which the
ontology-guided hybrid is built.
